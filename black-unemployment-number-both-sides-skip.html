The Black Unemployment Number Both Sides Skip

CE Field Guides · Published August 1, 2026

What a Field Guide is: we strip the headline down to the incentive structure underneath it. Who built it, who gains, who pays, what happens next. This one starts with two press releases that disagree and ends at the one source both of them are quoting without naming.

Two press releases went out this week about Black unemployment. One came from the National Urban League. One came from the White House. They do not agree. Both numbers are real. Neither institution is lying. That's the part worth sitting with.

The Two Numbers

The Urban League's 50th annual State of Black America report, released July 30, says 250,000 to more than 300,000 Black women lost their jobs in 2025. Separate reporting on the same document puts the toll on Black men at 567,000 jobs lost since November 2025. The White House told The Hill something different: Black unemployment fell 0.6 points in May, and Black employment rose by 101,000 between April and May.

The Urban League is measuring a full year. The White House is measuring two months. Pick your window, pick your headline.

The Source Nobody Named

Here's what neither press release says. The number both sides are quoting comes from the same place. The Joint Center for Political and Economic Studies publishes a Black unemployment breakdown every month, within days of each BLS jobs report. The White House's 101,000 figure is pulled straight from it. Nobody in the coverage said so.

Nobody mentioned the other half either. Joint Center published its own standalone report in January, "State of the Dream 2026," using the same monthly data. It already called 2025 a Black recession. It found Black unemployment at 7.5% by year's end, and it found Black women's unemployment rate higher than Black men's at that point, which is not the usual pattern. It tied that specifically to federal job cuts. That's independent evidence behind the Urban League's headcount claim, published six months earlier, by an organization that isn't the Urban League. Nobody connected the two.

The Volatility

Pull the monthly numbers and the picture gets less convenient for everyone:

March 2026: 7.1%. April 2026: 7.3%. May 2026: 6.6%.

That's not a trend. That's noise with a direction attached after the fact, depending on who's talking. A 0.7-point swing in two months is bigger than the improvement the White House cited. It's also bigger than the decline the Urban League's press cycle implies week to week. Whoever tells you the number is moving one clean direction is reading you one month.

What to Watch

Jointcenter.org's monthly Jobs Day Analysis, posted within days of each BLS release. It's the primary source both institutions are drawing from, and it doesn't need either of them to interpret it for you. Track the trend, not the print. One month of data is a data point. Three months in the same direction is a signal. Neither side currently has three months in the same direction to point to.

The next fight over this number is already scheduled. It happens every month, the day BLS drops the jobs report, whether or not anyone puts out a press release about it.
