Who Owns ALLBLK

Chartreuse Explosives · Published October 1, 2026

What Huh? is: the same sourcing as a Field Guide, with a lighter step. We follow one story until something in the paperwork makes us stop. This time it was a streaming service built for Black viewers that has changed owners twice and still has no public price tag.

On September 16, MACRO bought ALLBLK. The announcement named two financial advisors and two law firms. It did not name a price.

Charles D. King's company makes films like Judas and the Black Messiah. ALLBLK is the streaming service for Black TV and film that Bob Johnson started in 2014. The seller was AMC Global Media, the company behind The Walking Dead. Baller Alert called it the platform landing back with a Black owner after eight years under AMC.

That is the headline. We wanted the paperwork underneath it.

We have a soft spot for this one. In March 2006 The New York Times wrote about our founder's blog for calling the death of broadcast TV, months before Netflix streamed a single show. Eight years later the man who built BET carved a Black lane into streaming. This is how that lane changed hands.

2014: Johnson Builds The Lane

On November 5, 2014, Johnson's RLJ Entertainment launched UMC, the Urban Movie Channel. It lived on one website. Free trial through February 4, 2015. Then $2.99 a month to start, $4.99 after that.

The launch library was whatever RLJE already had in the vault. Stand-up from Kevin Hart, Jamie Foxx and Charlie Murphy. The documentary Dark Girls. A Bernie Mac tribute. 50 Cent in All Things Fall Apart. David E. Talbert's stage play What My Husband Doesn't Know.

Johnson's pitch was distribution without gatekeepers. Black creators reach Black viewers directly, outside the old development and distribution system. He had sold BET to Viacom in 2001. In 2015 he told the Bay State Banner the goal was "sort of a BET in the Digital Age."

The name drew fire on day one. A commenter on Lipstick Alley wrote that "urban" already put Black viewers in a box. It took six years and a new owner for the company to agree.

2016: Layaway

RLJE was a public company, and it was losing money. In the first half of 2016 it lost $13.6 million on $33.5 million in revenue. It carried about $61 million in debt.

On October 17, 2016, AMC Networks lent RLJE $65 million. The loan came with warrants that could convert into 50.1% of the company. SEC filings said the money would refinance the existing debt. The loans later grew to $78 million.

A loan with conversion warrants is buying a company on layaway.

AMC put the deposit down in 2016. It picked up the merchandise in 2018.

2018: The House And What Came With It

In February 2018 AMC offered $4.25 a share for the RLJE stock it did not already own. AMC held about 26% at that point. Johnson held about 47%.

In July the two sides settled at $6.25 a share. About $65 million in cash went to outside shareholders, on an enterprise value of roughly $274 million. Johnson and his affiliates kept 17%. The deal closed November 1, 2018.

Here is the detail that made us stop. RLJE ran two streamers. UMC was one. Acorn TV, the British mystery service, was the other. RLJE also owned 64% of Agatha Christie Ltd. AMC's CEO singled out the Christie stake as the IP opportunity. The filings reported the two streamers' subscribers together, more than 900,000 by the end of September 2018, and never split them.

Our read: UMC entered AMC as one line inside a deal whose headline assets were Poirot and Miss Marple. No public document says what AMC thought the Black streamer was worth on its own.

Most retellings say AMC bought UMC in 2018. It bought control of UMC's parent, after two years as its lender and part-owner. UMC came with the house.

The AMC Years

Brett Dismuke arrived in January 2019 and later ran both UMC and AMC's WE tv. The service built a slate of originals: Bronx SIU, A House Divided, Craig Ross Jr.'s Monogamy, Double Cross, Terror Lake Drive. A House Divided ran five seasons, the longest of any.

On January 6, 2021, UMC became ALLBLK. Dismuke told Variety that "urban" had been the industry's catch-all "so that we wouldn't necessarily have to say, the term 'Black.'" RLJE's CEO said the new brand targeted Black Americans, specifically Black women. Variety put the core audience at Black women 25 to 64, with the sweet spot at 28 to 38.

AMC said the service had grown by triple digits. It never gave the number it grew from.

In May 2022 the service launched in Canada and the Caribbean as ALLWAYSBLK. The library filled out with AMC's own WE tv reality shows, including Love After Lockup and The Braxtons. The price climbed to $7 a month.

The competition was family. On September 19, 2019, Viacom and Tyler Perry launched BET+. From then on, the streamer started by BET's founder was fighting the streamer that carried BET's name.

Seven Weeks After The Walking Dead Check

On April 8, 2026, AMC Networks renamed itself AMC Global Media. It said streaming was now its leading source of domestic revenue. Its streaming services ended 2025 with 10.4 million subscribers combined. ALLBLK's share was not broken out.

On July 30, AMC announced a deal licensing the entire Walking Dead universe to Netflix for $500 million over five years.

Seven weeks later it sold ALLBLK.

We do not know if the two are connected. AMC has not said, and the timing alone proves nothing.

The Buyer

King was the first Black partner at WME before he left to found MACRO in 2015. Emerson Collective led its first funding round. In 2017 MACRO raised $150 million in slate funding from Emerson and the Ford, Kellogg and Libra foundations. In March 2023 it closed a minority round of more than $90 million led by BlackRock, with HarbourView Equity Partners and Goldman Sachs Asset Management.

AMC was already on MACRO's investor list. AMC's CEO, Kristin Dolan, said it has backed MACRO for nearly a decade.

The film record: Fences, Mudbound, Just Mercy, They Cloned Tyrone, and Judas and the Black Messiah, the first Best Picture nominee with an all-Black producing team. King has said 80% of MACRO's projects recouped or made money. That figure is his.

His stated reason for the deal is consolidation. He called ALLBLK "a focused platform with a clear audience." MACRO already develops, finances and produces. Owning ALLBLK adds a monthly bill paid straight to MACRO, and a place to send its own projects without a studio or a global streamer in the middle.

Whose Money, Whose Ownership

Eleven years, three owners. First a public company, with Johnson the largest holder. Then AMC, first as lender, then as owner. AMC is controlled by the Dolan family. Now MACRO, Black-founded and Black-led, with minority investors that include BlackRock, Goldman Sachs Asset Management, HarbourView, Emerson Collective, three foundations, and AMC.

The "back with a Black owner" line holds for leadership. For equity we cannot check it. MACRO is private. Its ownership split is not public. Neither is how it paid for ALLBLK.

AMC keeps two seats at the table. It owns part of the buyer. And under a new licensing agreement, it gets paid to keep supplying shows to the service it just sold. Which shows, and whether the WE tv catalog stays, is not public.

Then there is Johnson. He kept 17% of RLJE in 2018. We looked for whether he still held a stake in 2026, whether ALLBLK still sat inside RLJE when it sold, and whether he had said anything about the sale. We found nothing on any of the three.

What They Would Say

We have not put these questions to AMC, MACRO or Johnson yet. What follows is reconstructed from their public statements and labeled that way.

AMC's case, reconstructed: it rescued an indebted company, paid for originals, retired the "urban" label, grew the service, and handed it to a Black-led partner it has backed for years, while keeping licensing revenue. Does it hold? Partly. The financing, the originals and the rebrand are on the record. The growth claim has no numbers attached. Whether AMC sold because MACRO can grow ALLBLK or because AMC was narrowing its focus, we do not know.

MACRO's case, reconstructed: consolidation is folding small streamers into big ones, which makes a focused service with a defined audience cheap to buy, and MACRO can feed it content AMC would not. Does it hold? The audience is documented. Whether a $7 standalone service for one audience can survive is not. Baller Alert notes that audience-specific services have been absorbed or shut down over the past three years. By its own announcement, this is MACRO's first move into direct-to-consumer streaming.

The Open File

What we pulled: AMC's SEC filings and press releases from 2016, 2018 and 2026. The 2014 launch coverage. The 2020 rebrand coverage. Every trade report on the 2026 sale. We searched for an ALLBLK subscriber count. Deadline reports that none has been disclosed lately.

What is still open:

  • What MACRO paid, and how it paid for it.
  • How many people subscribe to ALLBLK. We found no standalone figure, past or present.
  • Which titles AMC's licensing agreement covers, and whether WE tv stays.
  • Whether Johnson held any stake when ALLBLK sold.

Corrections To The Record

Several write-ups of the 2026 sale, including AfroTech's and Wikipedia's ALLBLK entry, say UMC launched in 2014 as a joint venture between RLJE and AMC. The 2014 launch coverage names RLJE alone. AMC's own filing dates its first investment to October 17, 2016.

The Hollywood Reporter dated that investment to November 2016. We use the filing's date.

Words We Used

Warrants: the right to buy a company's shares later at a set price. AMC's warrants let it turn its loan into majority control.

Enterprise value: what a whole company is worth to a buyer, counting its debt as well as its stock.

Licensing agreement: a deal where one company pays another for the right to show its content. Here, ALLBLK's new owner pays AMC for AMC shows.
